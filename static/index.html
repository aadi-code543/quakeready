    How QuakeReady works
    1. Detect. An open-vocabulary YOLO model finds furniture by name, including bookshelves, dressers and water heaters that normal detectors can't.
    2. Measure. A door (almost always 80 in tall) or the typical sizes of everything in view sets the photo's scale.
    3. Physics. A box of depth D and height H starts to tip when the floor accelerates sideways faster than g × D / H.
    4. Your shaking. USGS seismic design maps give the peak ground acceleration at your address. Upper floors shake harder (building code factor 1 + 2z/h).
    5. Fall zones. A tipping object lands about one height from its base. If that covers a bed, couch or door, it moves up the list.
    6. Rocking simulation. Starting to tip isn't the same as falling. Each object is rocked through 24 computer-generated earthquakes using Housner's rocking-block equations, and we count how often it actually falls over. The shake animation plays one of those quakes.
    7. Budget plan. A knapsack algorithm picks the set of fixes that raises your score the most for the money you have.
